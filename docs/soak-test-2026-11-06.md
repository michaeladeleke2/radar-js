# USB loss soak test, 2026-11-06

Question: why radar-js lost data (CRC errors, sync losses) while the Infineon
SDK did not, and why the 4b–4e session lost 123 times while idle and never
while recording. Five runs of 5 minutes, board and hub untouched, no
recording to file. radar-js `3b46dfc` (page-state logging added to the demo
only; no library change during the test). Same setup as
`docs/verification-summary.md` (BGT60TR13C, MCU7 firmware 2.9.0, USB 2.0 hub
with no other devices, MacBook Pro M4, macOS 27.2.0, Chrome 156.0.8078.48).

Data: `test/captures/soak/` (not committed): `soak1_sdk.sdk_soak.json`
(`tools/sdk_soak.py`) and the demo's session logs `soak2_plot_front`,
`soak3_plot_covered`, `soak4_plotoff_front`, `soak5_plotoff_covered`
`.session.json` (cumulative: the last holds runs 2–5). Frames expected come
from the board clock (firmware timestamps) for radar-js and from elapsed time
for the SDK.

acquisition failures, arrival gaps 77.5–137.3 ms (std 1.3 ms), 9.998 frames/s.

Realignment gaps: every one is about 0.13 s plus a whole number of seconds
(0 to 4), except one at 1.52 s. Almost all frames missing are those whole
seconds: 188 of 193, 323 of 335, 60 of 61, 36 of 39.

## Conclusions

1. **The bytes are lost below the page.** Where a gap lies inside one chunk
   Chrome handed to the page (25 cases), the bytes were already missing; the
   rest fit the same pattern. radar-js's parser and read loop do not drop
   data. No case had bytes added. Arbitrary byte counts (not whole USB
   packets) point to a byte buffer overflowing between the board and
   Chrome's serial reader (the link has no flow control); that is the most
   likely mechanism, not proven.
2. **The rate depends on whether Chrome is the frontmost app, not on the
   plot or on page visibility.** Focused: 9 losses in 9.1 minutes (runs 2
   untouched part and 4), about 1 per minute, with the plot on or off.
   Not focused: 56 in 10.2 minutes (runs 3 and 5), about 5.5 per minute,
   whether "visible" or "hidden". Run 2's first 52 s, switching between
   Chrome and the chat, had 18. This explains the 4b–4e pattern: during
   recordings Chrome was focused (Record had just been clicked); while idle
   the operator was usually in the chat app.
3. **radar-js loses more than the SDK even in its best case.** Focused and
   untouched: 9 losses in 9.1 minutes; the SDK: 0 in 5 minutes today and 0 in
   1,200 frames on 2026-11-05 (if both had the same rate, all 9 falling in the
   radar-js time has a probability of about 0.02). The SDK is a process that
   reads in a tight loop; Chrome reads through its browser process, whose
   scheduling macOS lowers for background apps.
4. **Most missing frames come from slow recovery, not from the drops.** A
   drop costs about 0.13 s when the restart goes cleanly, and one full second
   more for each control reply that never arrives: `Link.request` waits in 1 s
   steps while data keeps arriving (`src/device/link.js`). After a CRC error
   the parser is out of step and, at each sync loss, discards all buffered
   bytes (as the SDK's BridgeSerial does), which can include the board's
   reply to the restart's commands. With every realignment at 0.13 s, the
   radar-js runs would have lost about 5, 12, 1 and 3 frames instead of 193,
   335, 61 and 39 (the frames missing outside the whole-second part of the
   gaps).

Not settled: the 1.52 s gap; why run 5's restarts were mostly fast; whether
the hub plays a part (direct USB not tested); other Macs and Edge.

## Proposed changes (not made; waiting for the owner's OK)

A. **Faster recovery** (library, `src/device/link.js`, Strata-derived): on a
   sync loss, look for the next valid packet start in the bytes already
   buffered (a data header with the expected channel and counter, or a
   control response) instead of discarding them all, so the restart's replies
   are not thrown away; and stop waiting once the board has acknowledged the
   stop. Expected: most realignments at about 0.13 s, frames missing down from
   2–10% to well under 1% at today's loss rates. Needs fake-board tests
   (reply inside a misaligned stream) and the alignment test to stay green.

B. **Keep Chrome in front while recording** (demo only): record the page
   focus and visibility in every recording's sidecar, and show a warning when
   the page loses focus during a recording. Also add a read-size histogram to
   the session log, to tell whether the small reads seen near losses are
   normal.

C. **One more test, no code**: the same runs with the board plugged in
   directly (no hub), to rule the hub in or out.

Not proposed: moving the read loop into a Web Worker or enlarging Chrome's
buffer (already 16 MiB, the maximum). The bytes are lost before the page
sees them, so neither would help.

- **Recovery**: realignments now take 0.12–0.16 s (about one frame period)
  instead of 0.13 s plus 1–4 s; frames missing fell from 6.6% and 10.1% to
  0.09% and 0.75%, frames missing per drop from 8–9 to 0.4–2.1. The one slow
  realignment left (1.99 s, run 3b) came in a burst of 5 CRC errors in 0.3 s;
  a reply was probably lost to the byte loss itself rather than discarded by
  the parser (inferred, not shown).
- **Loss rate**: the change is not meant to alter it, and it did not in the
  focused run (1.4 / min, against 0.7–1.2 / min focused before). Run 3b's
  lower rate than run 3 (2.2 against 7.3 / min) is not a like-for-like
  comparison: covering Chrome made it "hidden" in 3b and only unfocused in 3
  (run 5, hidden and unfocused, had 3.4 / min).
- **Counters**: "sync lost" now counts once per misalignment (8 and 12,
  against 84 and 173), and the packets skipped while resynchronising show up
  as packet gaps (8 and 10, against 2 and 6).
